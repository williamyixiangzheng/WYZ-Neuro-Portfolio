Circuit mechanisms for disentangled representations and generalization in the hippocampus

Nature Neuroscience (2026), in press, Cornell University Tang, W.*, Chang, H.*, Liu, C., Perez-Hernandez, S., Zheng, Y. W., Park, J., Oliva, A., Fernández-Ruiz, A. * Equal contribution

Question

How does the hippocampus organize heterogeneous and dynamic single-neuron activity into coherent population representations that both distinguish individual experiences and support rapid generalization? Individual hippocampal neurons often exhibit mixed selectivity and changing activity over time, making it unclear how stable memory representations emerge at the population level. This study investigates how hippocampal circuits construct representations that preserve the uniqueness of individual experiences while extracting shared structure that enables rapid and flexible generalization.

Why it matters

Memory requires balancing two competing demands: maintaining the details that distinguish individual experiences while identifying relationships that can be applied to new situations. Understanding how hippocampal population activity achieves this balance provides insight into the neural computations underlying learning, memory organization, and flexible behavior.

Findings

The study found that hippocampal population activity forms structured, low-dimensional representations that capture relationships across related experiences. These population codes support generalization from limited experience, suggesting that the hippocampus builds abstract relational representations that can guide behavior in new situations.

Temporal and laminar organization of linguistic feature encoding in human single neurons

Manuscript in prep., Harvard Medical School & Massachusetts General Hospital Hardstone, R., Munoz, W., …, Zheng, Y. W., …, Williams, Z. (2026).

Question

How does the human brain transform linguistic knowledge into speech? We investigate which phonetic, syntactic, semantic, and behavioral features predict single-neuron activity, how strongly they modulate firing, and how their encoding and modulation are organized across time, cortical layers, and neural populations.

Why it matters

Distinguishing predictive encoding from firing-rate modulation is essential for understanding how linguistic representations are converted into spoken behavior. Revealing their temporal and laminar organization may also inform speech neuroprostheses and brain-computer interfaces.

Current progress

We are combining cross-validated single-neuron GLMs with population-level firing-modulation analyses to determine which linguistic and behavioral features predict neural activity, how strongly they alter firing rates, and how these effects vary across participants, temporal offsets, cortical layers, and feature families.

Topological evolution of hippocampal–prefrontal manifolds during long-term spatial memory consolidation

Ongoing project, Undergraduate honors thesis, Cornell University Zheng, Y. W., Park, J., Fernández-Ruiz, A., Oliva, A. (2026).

Question

How do hippocampal and prefrontal population representations transform as isolated spatial experiences are progressively integrated into a coherent long-term memory? Specifically, how are local route representations geometrically aligned, stabilized, and reorganized into a global cognitive map across extended learning?

Why it matters

Long-term memory requires more than preserving individual experiences; the brain connects related experiences into structured representations that support flexible recall, planning, and generalization. Identifying how hippocampal-prefrontal neural manifolds evolve and align over time may reveal the population-level mechanisms through which transient spatial representations become stable cortical schemas.

Current progress

We are developing manifold-learning, representational-alignment, decoding, and topological analyses to quantify how hippocampal-prefrontal population representations evolve across routes, learning stages, and extended experience.

A socio-spatial cognitive map of a naturalistic environment

Manuscript in prep., Cornell University Paudel, P., Zhao, Z., Ryan, J., Zheng, Y. W., Yu, A., Niraula, R., Zhao, E., Mei, X., Shen, J., Fernandez-Ruiz, A., and Oliva, A. (2026).

Question

How does the hippocampus construct spatial and social cognitive maps to guide flexible behavior in natural environments? Animals form territories, navigate to behaviorally relevant locations and adapt to other individuals, but how these experiences are represented in hippocampal-prefrontal circuits remains unclear.

Why it matters

Cognitive maps are thought to organize knowledge beyond physical space, yet their role in natural social behavior remains poorly understood. Determining how hippocampal activity integrates location, social context and prior experience, and how these representations are reinstated during offline periods, could reveal how memory supports territorial organization, homing and adaptive social behavior.

Current progress

We are using large-scale wireless neural recordings with automated multi-animal identity tracking to relate hippocampal and cortical population activity to spatial behavior, social interactions, and their integration during naturalistic behavior.

Long-term, large-scale neural recordings across memory circuits with high-density flexible probes

Manuscript in prep., Cornell University Park, J., Zhao, Z., Karaba, L., Perez-Hernandez, S., Zheng, Y. W., Kwon, A., Oliva, A., and Fernandez-Ruiz, A. (2026).

Question

How can the same neurons and their representations be tracked across the timescales of long-term learning and memory? Flexible neural interfaces may overcome the mechanical instability and tissue responses that limit chronic recordings while preserving the temporal resolution required to resolve single-cell and circuit dynamics.

Why it matters

Understanding whether individual neurons and cell assemblies remain stable or reorganize over time requires recordings that distinguish biological change from electrode drift and recording instability. Long-term, high-density recordings across the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex could reveal how spatial representations and distributed memory circuits evolve over weeks to months.

Current progress

We are developing and validating flexible, high-density neural probes for chronic recordings across the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex. Ongoing work combines probe fabrication, longitudinal unit tracking, spatial firing analysis and histological assessment to evaluate recording yield, single-neuron stability and representational consistency over weeks to months, with comparisons to conventional rigid probes.

Muscle atrophy and gene-therapy research in Huntington's disease

Science Advances (2026), non-author contribution, Peking University Dai, Y., Abudujielili, Z., Ding, Y., Huang, W., Yin, J., Ou, L., Hu, J., Zheng, S., & Li, C. (2026).

Question

Can CRISPR-mediated elimination of mutant huntingtin produce durable therapeutic benefits when delivered before or after the onset of Huntington’s disease? A central challenge is whether permanent gene editing can suppress disease progression across clinically relevant stages while limiting the risks associated with prolonged Cas9 activity.

Why it matters

Huntington’s disease is caused by a toxic gain-of-function mutation in HTT, making the mutant gene a direct therapeutic target. Establishing whether a single intervention can provide sustained benefit after pathology and behavioral deficits have emerged, while minimizing off-target editing, is critical for translating genome editing into a viable treatment.

Findings

The study developed an AAV-delivered, self-inactivating CRISPR-Cas9 system that transiently expressed Cas9 while permanently disrupting human mutant HTT. A single treatment substantially reduced mutant huntingtin protein and aggregates and produced lasting improvements in neuropathology, motor function, body weight and survival in BAC226Q mice. Benefits were observed when treatment was delivered before, near or well after disease onset, providing preclinical evidence that durable mutant-HTT editing may remain effective across different stages of disease progression.

Drosophila O-GlcNAc transferase research

Peking University

Question

How does altered O-GlcNAc transferase function affect development, physiology, and behavior in Drosophila? This work used genetic crosses and behavioral phenotyping to investigate how changes in a conserved post-translational regulatory pathway influence organism-level traits.

Why it matters

O-GlcNAcylation regulates many cellular processes, but connecting molecular perturbations to behavioral and developmental phenotypes requires tractable in vivo models.

Findings

This early-stage project involved phenotype screening and behavioral assays to identify candidate variants for further genetic analysis.
