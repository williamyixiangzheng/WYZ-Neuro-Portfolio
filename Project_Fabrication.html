Longitudinal place cell recordings with high-channel count, flexible probes in freely-moving mice

Cornell University, manuscript in prep.

Scientific question

How can the same neurons and their representations be tracked across the timescales of long-term learning and memory? Conventional rigid silicon probes differ mechanically from brain tissue, contributing to micromotion, tissue responses and recording instability that can obscure whether changes in neural activity reflect biological reorganization or technical drift.

This project develops high-density flexible probes and longitudinal analyses to evaluate single-neuron and representational stability across memory-related circuits and behavioral environments over months.

Abstract

Chronic in vivo electrophysiology in freely moving small animals has enabled the study of the neural circuit mechanisms of behavior. Despite its high spatio-temporal resolution and recent advances in high-density silicon probes, stable tracking of the same neurons across long periods of time has remained a challenge. This limitation has prevented the study of the neural mechanisms of learning and memory processes that unfold over multiple days or weeks. To overcome this barrier, we have implemented several technical innovations: 1) high-channel count, ultra-high density, flexible neural probes, 2) miniaturized active electronics, 3) analytical approaches to reliably track the same neurons across months. We integrated this technology into a scalable platform and performed longitudinal, large-scale recordings of the same hippocampal place cells and cortical cell assemblies over months in freely-moving mice.

Flexible probes were fabricated in a 5-µm-thick parylene-C thin film and contained PEDOT:PSS coated Pt 64 recording sites per shank with a double-sided architecture. This design resulted in the largest electrode density to date enabling us to record large numbers of neurons across different brain areas, and stably track them over months.

We integrated the flexible probes with a 128-channel CMOS-based unit module and implemented a scalable headstage architecture. Multiple modules can be connected in parallel to expand the system to 512 channels. A single 128-channel unit weighs ~0.8 g, and the full 512-channel headstage weighs approximately ~4.4 g; our system supports 512-channel recording through a single USB-C tether, reducing cable torque, tether drag, and head-pulling artifacts. Using this platform, we recorded from large numbers of hippocampal place cell and cortical neurons during mouse navigation to investigate the dynamics of representational drift within and across regions. We compared these dynamics with the structure of cell assemblies and pyramidal-interneuron monosynaptic interactions to elucidate how functional circuit architecture constrains representational dynamics over time. This platform provides a new approach for chronically tracking distributed memory-circuit dynamics across single-neuron, local micro-circuit, and brain-wide network scales during learning.
