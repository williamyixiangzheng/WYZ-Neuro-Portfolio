Software & Computational Work

Much of this work supports ongoing research and cannot be released publicly. The descriptions below therefore emphasize the computational challenges, software contributions, and validation principles rather than unpublished analyses or project-specific results.

Computational work

Statistical modeling of human single-neuron activity

Harvard Medical School & Massachusetts General Hospital, Private research code

I develop and maintain analysis tools for investigating how human single-neuron activity relates to linguistic and behavioral variables across time.

My contributions include:

  • Constructing analysis-ready representations from neural and behavioral annotations.
  • Aligning experimental events with neural activity across defined temporal relationships.
  • Implementing cross-validated statistical models and matched permutation controls.
  • Maintaining consistent neuron identities across model outputs and firing-rate analyses.

Technologies: Python, NumPy, pandas, SciPy, matplotlib, MATLAB, Linux and Git

Neural population geometry and topology

Cornell University · Private research code

I develop computational analyses for studying how neural population representations change across learning, behavioral conditions and recording sessions.

This work uses dimensionality reduction, manifold alignment, population decoding and topological methods to quantify representational structure. A central challenge is comparing recordings made on different days when the observed neuronal populations may not be identical. I therefore focus on matched sampling, appropriate null models and sensitivity analyses that separate biological reorganization from changes in data quality or population composition.

Methods: PCA, UMAP, CEBRA, Procrustes analysis, persistent homology, population decoding and cross-session comparison

Naturalistic behavior and neural-data integration

Cornell University · Private research code

I contribute to systems that reconstruct the behavior of multiple freely moving animals and synchronize those measurements with large-scale neural recordings.

My work includes processing pose and object detections, integrating trajectory and inertial measurements, extracting spatial and social variables, and aligning behavioral streams with electrophysiological data. These systems support analyses of navigation, territorial organization and social interaction in environments that are less constrained than conventional laboratory tasks.

Technologies: Python, DeepLabCut, YOLO, inertial measurement units, Arduino and MATLAB

Software-development principles

Across these projects, I emphasize:

  • Version-controlled and modular analysis code.
  • Scripted data processing and figure generation.
  • Explicit parameter and provenance tracking.
  • Automated validation and quality-control output.
  • Statistical controls defined alongside the primary analysis.
  • Separation of intermediate data, summary tables and presentation figures.
  • Documentation that allows analyses to be repeated and reviewed.

These practices are particularly important for longitudinal and large-scale analyses, where results may depend on preprocessing decisions made across many recording sessions.
