Cortical laminar architecture and neuronal dynamics of speech production and comprehension in humans

Harvard Medical School & Massachusetts General Hospital, manuscript in prep.

Hardstone, R., Munoz, W., …, Zheng, Y. W., …, Williams, Z. (2026).

Background

Human sentence construction requires the rapid integration of phonological, lexical, syntactic, and predictive information before speech. Although the posterior middle frontal gyrus (pMFG) has been implicated in language production, it remains unclear how linguistic information is organized across cortical layers and over time.

We recorded single-unit spiking activity and local field potentials (LFPs) from the posterior middle frontal gyrus while participants constructed sentences from visual scenes. We asked whether models of linguistic features could predict neural activity at distinct temporal profiles and across cortical layers.

Scientific question

How are linguistic representations organized over time and across cortical layers during human sentence construction?
